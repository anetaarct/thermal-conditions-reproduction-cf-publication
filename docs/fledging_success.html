Fledging success

The specifications, estimates and checks below describe the unchanged temperature-association model. Its year coefficient is adjusted for temperature and is retained for full disclosure, rather than reported as the temporal trend. Temporal change is estimated using a separate model without temperature, presented in the Temporal trends section.

1 S1.7 Fledging success: final ML estimates and diagnostics

2 Primary structure and numerical verification.

The primary fledging model was fitted by maximum likelihood in glmmTMB 1.1.14 to the same 9,790 brood records. Mean and log-precision predictors, links and random effects were retained from the former ML fit; the only extension was an independent year random intercept in logit extra-zero probability. An initial unrestricted nlminb attempt failed with NA/NaN gradients and was excluded. A scaled, bounded L-BFGS-B fit yielded an interior solution (minimum relative distance to bounds 0.261). Independent unbounded BFGS refinement gave convergence code zero and a positive-definite Hessian; the two log likelihoods agreed within \(10^{-7}\) and maximum absolute SE-scaled gradients were below \(3.8\times10^{-4}\). Log likelihood was \(-16632.0562\), with 15 parameters and AIC 33294.11, versus 33463.58 for the constant-extra-zero ML model (14 parameters). We report AIC rather than an ordinary chi-square likelihood-ratio p-value because the null random-effect variance lies on a boundary. This comparison concerns fitted marginal likelihood, not out-of-sample validation.

3 Reference predictions.

Curves use ML point estimates and pointwise 95% CIs from 8,000 joint asymptotic coefficient draws, including covariance across location, precision and extra-zero coefficients. Every random effect is set to zero, including precision-year and extra-zero-year effects. We computed \(\mu=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(X\widehat\beta)\), \(\phi=\exp(Z\widehat\gamma)\) and \(\pi_0=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\widehat\alpha_0)\) explicitly, and verified them against native predictions with grouping variables set to missing. In glmmTMB 1.1.14, re.form=NA alone did not remove precision-year and extra-zero-year contributions for an existing year. Explicit fixed designs prevent these contributions entering reference curves. Curves are conditional reference predictions, not averages over the distribution of years or individual-brood prediction intervals.

4 Random effects and extra-zero probability.

Primary location SDs were 0.3003 for females (approximate 95% CI 0.2119–0.4254) and 0.3096 for years (0.2395–0.4003). Year SD was 0.2490 in log precision (0.1611–0.3849) and 0.5261 in logit extra-zero probability (0.4025–0.6876). Intervals were obtained on the log-SD scale and back-transformed. At zero extra-zero-year effect, \(\pi_0=0.1747\) (95% CI 0.1500–0.2026); this is neither mean annual extra-zero probability nor total failure probability.

5 Primary simulation checks and limitations.

We generated 1,000 conditional plug-in datasets at estimated parameters and fitted group modes and 1,000 new-group plug-in datasets with newly drawn random effects, using the original covariates and clutch-size denominators. Matched comparisons used seed 20261020 for both ML structures. Overall observed mean success (0.5861), zero fraction (0.2365) and complete-success fraction (0.2886) lay within the primary conditional simulation intervals. Conditional variance was slightly underestimated: observed 0.15455 versus simulation mean 0.15191 and 95% interval 0.14962–0.15430. All four overall statistics were covered by new-group plug-in intervals. These intervals omit parameter-estimation uncertainty; a refit-based bootstrap was not performed. Coverage at a single 95% threshold is descriptive and subject to Monte Carlo variation.

Conditional annual zero frequencies lay within simulation intervals in all 39 seasons, versus 16/39 seasons outside for the former constant-extra-zero ML fit in this matched rerun. For new-group simulations the corresponding counts outside were 3/39 and 13/39. Conditional annual means and variances were all covered, but complete-success frequency was outside in 1/39 years. Group discrepancies remained: conditional zero frequencies were outside in 1/2 age classes and 4/5 laying-date groups; means were outside in 1/2 age classes and 4/5 date groups, and variances in 1/2 age classes and 3/5 date groups. These are correlated descriptive checks, not independent adjusted tests. Calibration using fitted year effects is in-sample and cannot establish performance for unseen seasons. The extra-zero-year effect is statistical heterogeneity, not identification of a biological mechanism of complete failure.

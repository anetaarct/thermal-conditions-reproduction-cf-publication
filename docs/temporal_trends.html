Temporal trends without temperature

1 Two different questions

Temporal change: how did mean reproductive performance and reproductive consistency change over calendar time? These models were refitted without temperature in both location and scale. Age class, relative laying date, the original response distributions and all random-effect structures were retained.

Temperature associations: how were the reproductive traits associated with spring temperature? These use the unchanged models containing temperature and calendar year together. Their temperature-adjusted year coefficients are disclosed on the model pages but are not used as the reported temporal trends. The temperature, laying-date and age results and their figures remain unchanged.

2 Temporal results

At yearling age, relative laying date zero and all random effects zero:

Trait Period Expected first → last value Change (95% CI)
Clutch size 1981–2025 5.84 → 6.26 eggs +0.43 eggs (+0.26 to +0.60)
Egg-to-fledging success 1981–2019 67.5% → 57.2% −10.3 percentage points (−16.3 to −4.1)

Evidence of temporal change in reproductive consistency was inconclusive for both traits: the first-to-last clutch-size SD ratio was 1.063 (95% CI 0.994–1.136), and the fledging precision ratio was 0.780 (0.513–1.189). Both intervals include one. Lower clutch-size SD indicates greater consistency; higher fledging precision indicates greater consistency at a fixed component mean and clutch size.

These trends are not adjusted for temperature, but are standardised for age and relative laying date. They are not unadjusted population trajectories and do not identify how much change was caused by warming. The two traits also cover different periods. The observed annual means are supplied separately for descriptive comparison.

3 Models and uncertainty

Clutch size uses the mean-parameterised COM-Poisson distribution, with female and year random intercepts in location and no year random intercept in scale. Fledging success uses the zero-inflated beta-binomial distribution, with female and year random intercepts in location and separate year random intercepts in precision and extra-zero probability. The extra-zero equation has no covariate slopes. No interactions are included.

Both temporal models converged with positive-definite Hessians. Their log likelihoods were reproduced within 10⁻⁷ by BFGS refinement without the initial Hessian-based coordinate transformation. Clutch size: 10 parameters, log likelihood −13776.6071, AIC 27573.21. Fledging: 13 parameters, log likelihood −16637.7042, AIC 33301.41. AICs for the unchanged temperature-association models were 27561.10 and 33294.11. These comparisons describe fit and do not replace the distinction between the scientific questions.

Pointwise 95% CIs for temporal curves use 6,000 joint asymptotic coefficient draws, retaining cross-component covariance. Clutch-size SD is computed from the exact COM-Poisson distribution. Fledging mean success is the full expectation (1−π)μ; precision describes the conditional consistency measure rather than all variability in the mixture. Intervals concern expected curves and are not individual-brood prediction intervals.

4 Model checks and limitations

We simulated 500 conditional plug-in datasets per temporal model at estimated parameters and fitted group effects. These checks omit parameter-estimation uncertainty and do not assess held-out prediction. The exact COM-Poisson probability sampler was verified against the fitted mean and variance; a native new-group sampler returned non-finite values and its simulations were excluded. Conditional variance remained underestimated in both traits, while overall means and fledging complete-failure and complete-success frequencies fell within their simulation intervals. The distributional limitations documented on the clutch-size and fledging-success pages remain relevant. Both model sets use the same data and are not independent replication.
